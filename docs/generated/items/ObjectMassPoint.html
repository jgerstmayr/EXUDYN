ObjectMassPoint

A 3D mass point which is attached to a position-based node, usually NodePoint.

Simpler: mbs.CreateMassPoint adds this item, with what it needs, in one call.

Interface

  • Python names: ObjectMassPoint or MassPoint, and VMassPoint for its visualization

  • Nodes it takes: NodePoint, NodeRigidBodyEP, NodeRigidBodyRxyz, NodeRigidBodyRotVecLG, NodePointSlope1, NodePointSlope12, NodePointSlope23, NodePointGround

  • Body markers that can be placed on it: MarkerBodyMass, MarkerBodyPosition

Equations of motion

\[ \mr{m}{0}{0} {0}{m}{0} {0}{0}{m} \vr{\ddot q_0}{\ddot q_1}{\ddot q_2} = \vr{f_0}{f_1}{f_2} . \]

A LoadCoordinate on coordinate 1 of the node adds to \(f_1\).

Marker interfaces

The position of a local point is \(\LU{0}{\pv}\cConfig + \pLocB\), as the body frame is the global frame. The position Jacobian is the unit matrix,

\[ \LU{0}{\Jm_{pos}} = \frac{\partial \LU{0}{\vv}}{\partial \dot\qv} = \mr{1}{0}{0} {0}{1}{0} {0}{0}{1} , \]

so a global force acts as it is, \(\Qm = \LU{0}{\fv}_a\), and the mass-weighted Jacobian is \(m\) times it. A mass point has no rotation: there is no rotation Jacobian, and no MarkerBodyRigid fits.

Mini example

node = mbs.AddNode(NodePoint(referenceCoordinates = [1,1,0], 
                             initialCoordinates=[0.5,0,0],
                             initialVelocities=[0.5,0,0]))
mbs.AddObject(MassPoint(nodeNumber = node, mass=1))

#assemble and solve system for default parameters
mbs.Assemble()
mbs.SolveDynamic()

#check result
exu.sys['testResult'] = mbs.GetNodeOutput(node, exu.OutputVariableType.Position)[0]
#final x-coordinate of position shall be 2
