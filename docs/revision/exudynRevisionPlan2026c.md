# Revision plan how to

**The file** has four sections, each exactly once and in this order: *how to* (these rules), *next steps*
(hand-written), *open steps* (generated) and *groups* (a generated table, then the groups and their steps).
`python docs/revision/planManager.py` checks the plan, writes the generated parts, lists, shows, adds and changes
steps. **Run `planManager.py update` after every edit**; it must end with `OK`.

**A group or step is a heading** that starts with its marker and its title:

- `## **OPEN_RGx** Title` for a group, `### **OPEN_RGx.y** Title` for a step, `#### **CLOSED_RGx.y.z** Title` for
  a sub-step - the heading level follows from the number of parts;
- the marker is `OPEN_` or `CLOSED_`, and it appears **only** in that heading, so a search for `_RGx.y**` finds
  the step and `OPEN_RG` finds every open one;
- a step reaches from its heading to the next heading;
- **the title says what is done in the step, and how** - not why, not when, not what was there before.

**Status.** A group is open as long as one of its steps is open, and closed otherwise. A closed step has no open
step below it. A closed step keeps its title and one or two lines - date, issues, outcome; the details go to the log.

**Numbers are permanent.** `RGx.y.z`, given when the step is written down and never reused - also not a number that
revision2026b gave, which `planManager.py add` knows. The content of a step may change when the plan changes or a
measurement points elsewhere; its number does not. Groups RG1 to RG11 continue those of revision2026b with their
numbers; the higher groups of revision2026b are closed, and what was left of them is in RG11.

**Referring to a step**: `RG4.21` inside this plan, never with the marker; "revision2026c step RG4.21" outside it, and
"revision2026b step RG12.29" or "revision2026 step R4.3" for the closed plans. **Code, commits and documentation cite
the issue** (`#2884`), not the plan: the plan is internal.

**Issues carry the content.** A step is first written down with what is to be done. Once it has an issue, the issue
holds the description and the step keeps the issue number and a short line - plus what does not belong in an issue:
explanations, decisions of the maintainer, measurements that steer the plan.

**New groups and new steps at the level RGx.y** are proposed to the maintainer before they are written down;
sub-steps may be added directly.

**Next steps** are hand-written: the main open steps and the recommended next ones, usually with some that can be
done now without other people's time or open decisions. `planManager.py` checks that each step named there exists
and is open. **Open steps** and the **groups** table are generated - never edit them by hand.

##### **OPEN_RG4.1.3.8** Let Eigen multiply and add on arm64 instead of fusing, and verify it on macOS ARM

#2990. The macOS ARM run of 1.13.75 fails in six test models (createSphereQuadContact, createSphereTriangleContact,
sphereTriangleTest2, sliderCrank3Dbenchmark, and the listed ANCFgeneralContactCircle and
geometricallyExactBeamRightAngleFrame), all solved with `LinearSolverType.EigenSparse`. The dumps of the Mac agent
(tmp/MacOS/MacAgentResponse, `dumpRun.py`) against the same script on Windows: the inputs - initial and reference
coordinates, every item, the numbers numpy computes in sliderCrank3Dbenchmark - are the same bits, the explicit solve
of sphereTriangleTest2 too; the implicit solves part by one ulp in some step. Eigen's NEON packets fuse a*b+c
(`vfmaq_f64` under `__ARM_FEATURE_FMA`) whatever `-ffp-contract=off` says. LinearSolver.h undefines the macro around
the Eigen includes; done on Windows (no change there), open until the Mac run confirms; then `UnresolvedOnMacOS()`
shrinks. The same holds for the fast module, whose `-mfma` makes Eigen's AVX packets fuse (`__FMA__`) - a separate
decision, as that module is meant to be fast.

2026-10-13, the Mac run of the change (tmp/MacOS/MacAgentResponse2): clang's arm_neon.h needs the macro, so it is
included before the macro is undefined. With that, sliderCrank3Dbenchmark is bit-identical with Windows and
ANCFgeneralContactCircle passes; 7 of the 8 entries of `UnresolvedOnMacOS()` pass. Four models still differ:
createSphereQuadContact, createSphereTriangleContact and sphereTriangleTest2 (EigenSparse; from step 13, resp. after step
50, by one ulp in 32 of 49 coordinates at once) and geometricallyExactBeamRightAngleFrame (static, EigenSparse). Next:
the same dumps with `LinearSolverType.EXUdense` on both platforms (`tmp/MacOS/dumpRun2.py`, `compareDumps2.py`, the
Windows dumps in `WindowsReference2.zip`) - identical then means the rest is in Eigen too.
