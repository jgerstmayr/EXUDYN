# The C++ solvers

How the solvers of the C++ core are built: the files, the classes, the step loop, Newton, the step size control, the
output, the Python layer and the implicit Runge-Kutta solvers. What the solvers compute and how they are used is in
the user manual, {ref}`sec-solvers`.

The system side that the solvers call is `CSystem` (`src/Main/CSystem.h`): `ComputeSystemODE2RHS`, the ODE1 right-hand
side, the algebraic equations, `JacobianODE2RHS` (numerical or analytical per item), `NumericalJacobianAE`, the mass
matrix, the post-Newton functions of the items (`listDiscontinuousIteration`).

## 2. The classes

```
CSolverBase                                  data, it, conv, output, file, timer, newton, discontinuous, restart
 ├─ CSolverStatic                            load factor as time; Newton on the static residual
 ├─ CSolverImplicitSecondOrderTimeInt        generalized-alpha / Newmark / TrapezoidalIndex2
 │   │   has CSolverImplicitRungeKutta       the implicit Runge-Kutta solvers, run in place of Newton (section 7)
 │   └─ CSolverImplicitSecondOrderTimeIntUserFunction   each virtual function replaceable by a Python function
 └─ CSolverExplicitTimeInt                   RK tableaus, VelocityVerlet; Newton() is the explicit step
MainSolverBase  →  MainSolverStatic, MainSolverImplicitSecondOrder, MainSolverExplicit   (Python classes)
```

The data of a solver are the generated structures, public members of `CSolverBase`: `data` (`SolverLocalData`: sizes,
the system residual and Jacobian - dense or sparse `GeneralMatrix` -, the mass matrix, temporary vectors per thread,
`aAlgorithmic` of generalized-alpha), `it` (`SolverIterationData`: times, step sizes, counters, automatic step size
flags), `conv` (convergence flags and errors), `output`, `file`, `timer`. The state is not the solver's: it is
`CSystemData::cData`, with `currentState`, `startOfStepState`, `initialState` and `visualizationState`
(`CSystemState`: ODE2 coordinates and their two derivatives, ODE1 coordinates and velocities, AE and data
coordinates, time). A solver writes the current state, and the step loop copies it to the start of the step.

## 3. The step loop (CSolverBase::SolveSteps)

```
SolveSystem: InitializeSolver (pre-checks, data, output files, initial conditions: initial accelerations)
             SolveSteps; FinalizeSolver
SolveSteps:  FinishStep for the initial state (or the restart state)
             while not end time and not stopped:
                 startOfStepState = currentState
                 repeat until the step is accomplished or the step reduction failed:
                     InitializeStep              pre-step user function, UpdateCurrentTime (t += h)
                     DiscontinuousIteration      loop: Newton; FinalizeNewton; PostNewton (data coordinates)
                                                 - error > tolerance: back to the start state, again (or false
                                                   with a recommended smaller step)
                     failed: currentState = startOfStepState; ReduceStepSize (adaptiveStep, automaticStepSize)
                     done: FinishStep (post-step user function, solution and sensor files at the write periods,
                           visualization state, verbose output, real-time); after adaptiveStepRecoverySteps good
                           steps IncreaseStepSize
                 restart file at its write period
```

- **One step is one Newton solve** for the implicit solver: `Newton()` in the base class iterates
  `ComputeNewtonResidual` / `ComputeNewtonJacobian` / `ComputeNewtonUpdate` (virtual), with the modified Newton
  method (Jacobian kept over steps), the factorization of the linear solver, the divergence checks and the
  Jacobian update rules; the unknowns are the system coordinates `nSys = nODE2 + nODE1 + nAE` - for
  generalized-alpha the accelerations (ODE2), the ODE1 coordinates and the multipliers. The implicit Runge-Kutta
  solvers have more unknowns per step (stages, mu of GGL) and keep their own (section 7).
- **The explicit solver overrides `Newton()`** with the whole explicit step (the stages, the final combination, the
  error estimate); the base class loop, the discontinuous iteration and the output are shared.
- **The static solver** uses the same loop with the load factor as time.

## 4. Step size control

- **Implicit (generalized-alpha)**: no error estimate. `adaptiveStep`: the step is reduced when Newton fails or the
  post-Newton step recommends a smaller step (`it.recommendedStepSize`, `adaptiveStepDecrease`), and increased after
  `adaptiveStepRecoverySteps` steps with few iterations (`adaptiveStepIncrease`); `ReduceStepSize` and
  `IncreaseStepSize` of the solver class.
- **Explicit**: `automaticStepSize` for the tableaus with an embedded method (ODE23, DOPRI5): inside `Newton()` the
  error of the embedded solution in the RMS norm with `absoluteTolerance` and `relativeTolerance` (positions,
  velocities, ODE1), scaled by the absolute values; the step size by `StepSizeController` (below); a rejected step
  repeated at once, an accepted one gives the next step size (#2109, #3245).
- **`StepSizeController`** (`src/Solver/StepSizeController.h`) is the one step size rule of the adaptive solvers
  (ODE23, DOPRI5, TrapezoidalGGL, the implicit Runge-Kutta solvers): `Control` is the PI controller of Gustafsson with
  `stepSizeSafety`, `stepSizeMaxIncrease`, `minimumStepSize` and no growth after a rejection; `ReducedStepSize` is the
  step after a failed post-Newton step or Newton iteration - the recommended step size, at most 0.75 h, else
  `adaptiveStepDecrease` - also without growth afterwards (#3394); generalized-alpha uses `ReducedStepSize` as well.
  The Python solvers have the same rule in `_ErrorStepSize`.

## 5. Output and restart

- `FinishStep` writes the solution file (`WriteCoordinatesToFile`, text or binary) and the sensors
  (`WriteSensorsToFile`, files and internal storage under the lock of each sensor) at the multiples of the write
  periods: `WriteOutputWithinStep` sets the state interpolated between `startOfStepState` and the end of the step
  (`SetStateWithinStep`) for each multiple within the step, calls the writers and restores the end (#3286; Python
  #3241); the quintic Hermite polynomial, and the rotations of Lie group nodes in their Lie algebra (#3389). The visualization state is copied for the renderer; the pre- and post-step user functions
  are called in `InitializeStep` / `FinishStep`.
- The restart file (#2850): the state, the step index and step size, written at its period after a finished step;
  read and applied at the start of `SolveSteps`.
- `SolverOutputData` / the solver file: verbose output and statistics; the timer per phase (`CSolverTimer`).

## 6. The Python layer

- `MainSolverBase` exposes the C solver's phases to Python (`InitializeSolver`, `SolveSteps`, `Newton`,
  `ComputeNewtonResidual`, ... with a `MainSystem`), and its data structures (`it`, `conv`, `output`, `newton`,
  `timer`) as properties; the bindings are generated from `structureDefsSolvers.py`.
- `CSolverImplicitSecondOrderTimeIntUserFunction`: every virtual phase can be replaced by a Python function - used to
  write a solver in Python on the C++ loop (e.g. examples with a user Newton).
- `exudyn.solver.SolveDynamic` selects `MainSolverImplicitSecondOrder` or `MainSolverExplicit` by the solver type.
- The Python solvers of `exudyn.symbolics.solvers` and `.advancedSolvers` do not use the C++ loop: they have their own
  loop (`_StepSize`, `_ErrorStepSize`, `_Output`, `_PostNewton`), the same settings, and write through mbs
  (`WriteCoordinatesToFile`, `WriteSensorsToFile` of the Main solvers, #3241).

## 7. The implicit Runge-Kutta solvers

- **One algorithm, the method as data.** TrapezoidalGGL, LobattoIIIA4, LobattoIIIC4GGL, Radau3 and Radau5 are a stiffly
  accurate implicit Runge-Kutta method given by its tableau and the place of its constraints:
  `ImplicitRungeKuttaMethod {name, s, A, c, ggl, order}`, one table in `CSolverImplicitRungeKutta.cpp`. A further
  method is a table entry. The Python versions are `SolveTrapezoidalGGL` and `SolveImplicitRungeKutta` of
  `exudyn.symbolics.advancedSolvers`.
- **A component, not a subclass**: `CSolverImplicitRungeKutta` is a member of `CSolverImplicitSecondOrderTimeInt`,
  whose `Newton()` runs its `Step()` when `timeIntegration.solverType` is one of the five. So the step loop, the
  initial accelerations, the discontinuous iteration (the data coordinates by the post-Newton step after the stages
  converged), the output, the restart, `MainSolverImplicitSecondOrder` and the implicit branch of `SolveDynamic` are
  those of generalized-alpha.
- **Unknowns** per implicit stage: the accelerations (nODE2) and the ODE1 velocities (nODE1); with GGL the multipliers
  z and mu per implicit stage, with 'end' a multiplier per implicit stage and the two constraint levels at the end of
  the step. They are not `nSys`, so the class keeps its own vectors and its own `GeneralMatrix`, dense or sparse by
  `linearSolverType`.
- **Residual**: the stage states from the unknowns and the start of the step, then per implicit stage the residual of
  `CSystem` (the same calls as generalized-alpha) and the constraint rows; the time derivative of a constraint
  numerically.
- **Jacobian**: the system Jacobians (by q, by v, the mass matrix) at the last stage of the current iterate, placed
  into the stage matrix by the coefficients of A and A^2 as sparse triplets; the term (C_q v)_q is left out. mu is h^2
  times its unknown; the rows are scaled to entries of order 1 (ODE2 and ODE1 equations 1, constraints 1/h^2, velocity
  constraints 1/h); convergence by the norm of the unscaled residual, as generalized-alpha. No allocations per step:
  the work arrays of the stage Jacobian are kept (#3287).
- **Special rows**: velocity-level constraints (`UsesVelocityLevel`) and constraints whose equation is their
  multiplier (inactive ones) have their equation at each implicit stage with mu = 0 (GGL), or at the last two stages
  ('end').
- **Lie group nodes**: the rotation of a stage is the composition of the start of the step with the increment of the
  stage, and the stage Jacobian has the tangent operator of the last stage, as generalized-alpha (#3288, #3290).
- **Error estimate**, `timeIntegration.errorEstimate`: `oneStep` - the difference to the trapezoidal rule of the first
  and the last stage, for TrapezoidalGGL h^2/12 (a1 - a0) and the velocities by the mean accelerations; `twoSteps` -
  the quadrature of the method against the polynomial through the stage velocities of the previous and this step,
  which keeps the stages of the last accepted step, the first step by `oneStep`. Both in the error norm of the
  explicit solvers; the step size by `StepSizeController` (section 4).
- **Output within the step**: the quintic Hermite polynomial of the base class; the ODE1 coordinates by the collocation
  polynomial of the stages (#3379).
- **Verbose output**: level 3 prints the residual per block, level 4 checks the stage Jacobian against finite
  differences.
- The measured orders and times are in the user manual, {ref}`sec-solvers`, and in `python/testing/solverStudy.py` on
  `python/TestModels/modelLibrary.py`.
